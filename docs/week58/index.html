Hawaii Laie Mission — Week 58

Bruce and Sara Conrad, 2026

Sunday (July 26, 2026) morning we woke up early. Bruce walked past the zoo, and all the way along Monsarrat Avenue to the corner with Paki Avenue, where the car is parked in the very last available spot, hoping to be able to move it to a closer spot. No such luck: there is a bicycle race and a foot race and some kind of festival going on and there is no convenient parking. So, when he came back, he reserved overnight parking at the hotel. Meanwhile, Sara fixed a great breakfast. Phone call with Eli and Rod Meldrum, as Eli is organizing a mid-singles event in Idaho. Sara not feeling well, Bruce drove himself to church; the meeting was great, as was the following priesthood meeting. Right afterwards, he made it back to the hotel in half an hour; Sara was waiting and we drove to Whole Foods to eat. Bruce worked on a bit of personal history, from July 1, 2013. We fell asleep watching One-Eyed Jacks (1961).

Monday morning, we got to the sea early and it was very nice. View from balcony afterwards.

Sixth floor of Waikiki Grand Hotel facing Diamond Head, Honolulu Zoo, and the Pacific Ocean

We cleaned up and packed and hit the road at 9:25am, arriving home at 10am on the dot. Sara worked hard on chapter six and got 100% on the first quiz on the first try. Bruce napped and assisted. Laundry day.

Tuesday morning, Bruce awoke first around 5am. We fixed breakfast and got ready for the district council meeting at 10am. We have two new elders (one out less than a week) and three new sister missionaries. Bruce was humbled when the newest elder told us that he was experienced in ward missionary huddles, thinking that we hadn't done many of them in the few days he had been on his mission. Wrong; he was talking about the three years of experience he had as a called ward missionary in his home ward in Arizona. These youth are well-prepared!

In the afternoon, Sara completed another couple of quizzes, both with a 100% score on the first try. Bruce, meanwhile, caught up on his naps, and fiddled with a couple of his life history documents. Mike told us that the monthly Canadian dollar check has not yet arrived. It was late in January, too, but did come eventually. In an unprecedented turn of events, Bruce finished both weekly puzzles first.

Wednesday, Bruce woke up at 6:45am to a text from Elizabeth; Sara was asleep on the couch, so she must have awakened earlier then went back to sleep. After breakfast, Sara aced a couple more quizzes. After lunch, she was working on an assignment while Bruce napped and read and started writing another piece of his life history. Then, while Sara took a bath to cool down, he drove on some errands, selling cans and bottles ($3.10), looked in a thrift store for a specific canning jar, and got books at the library. We spoke on the phone and he returned to pick Sara up for a trip to the Kailua thrift store. We found the needed jar there! Then we ate at Whole Foods and bought some tomatoes and oatmeal soap. Home, tired by 6:30. Fell asleep to Sheep Detectives (2026).

Thursday morning, we both slept in a bit. Bruce left a message with Matt Greene. It is time to speak with him again about Sara's program, since she will soon be completing the Finance 300 course that will then need to be transferred in, to her BGS program at BYU.

Bruce had committed to participating in the third ward choir for a funeral. Flowers therefrom:

Foreground: beautiful anthuriums grown in the deceased's garden; her picture among orchids

The anthurium is Sara's favorite flower, motivating Bruce to take these pictures. It was an interesting funeral; with many non-member friends attending there was applause after most of the talks and musical numbers. The choir sang It Is Well With My Soul (an arrangement by Mack WIlberg). Bruce was struck by the Bishop bringing to light an acrostic in the last five words of 2 Nephi 9:39, "spiritually-minded is life eternal." Smile.

While he was gone, the sister missionaries dropped by to pick up some pamphlets and a repairman dropped in to see about installing a window-mounted air conditioner in our bedroom. As a result, Sara didn't go to the funeral. The person plans to return on Saturday to install it, but someone has to come and remove the window for him first. We tried to get Jasmin to do it, but Aaron dropped by with him and they determined that a professional window installer will have to be called in. Sara finished the last quiz for her class, missing only one question (in a quiz of twenty questions), for a score of 95%, which will give her an A for the entire class, once the manual grades come in for two small assignments. We celebrated by watching a couple of movies, A Perfect World (1993) and An Unfinished Life (2005) before drifting off to sleep.

Friday morning, Bruce was awakened by a phone call from Matt Greene, at 5:17am. This was delightful (and expected (although his phone had been set to do-not-disturb (so that's disturbing))). The call lasted 18 minutes and Matt laid out for him the remaining steps for Sara's bachelor's degree, including that she can begin the last class (a two credit capstone) right away, and that she'll be able to graduate in December. Kai Martinez came and removed the glass from a portion of our bedroom window. We paid the cost ($272.25) and will be reimbursed.

Left: a few weeks ago; Right: today, no glass in bottom-left pane, top-left pane slid open

We drove to lunch at Pan's Kitchen and then directly to the sisters' apartment for the monthly inspection that we are asked to perform. They have a cute tiny Etch-a-sketch that they had used to do an impression of the Hawaii Temple; Bruce remembers how difficult it is to create drawings on this device, and so wanted a picture.

Afterwards, we drove to Safeway and shopped the Friday specials. Home again and Helaman helped us carry everything upstairs. Our bedroom is so hot without an air conditioner, so we kept the door closed; the window of course is wide open with no glass at all. Sara took a long bath, while Bruce read and napped. Then she fixed a great meal of salad, olives, and cheesy pesto pasta. Bruce read on the porch until dark. During that time, she slid the top window closed (we hadn't been able to operate it until we removed the in-room air conditioner this morning (Bruce carried it down to the bulky items disposal site)). Sara is sleeping on the bed in the living room near that A/C, while Bruce is sleeping near the missing bedroom window with a box fan blowing outside (cooler) air in. He also has the exhaust fan running in the bathroom; we'll see how well it works, but it's only temporary because a new in-window air conditioner is slated for installation early tomorrow morning.

Saturday, Sara was awake first. After breakfast, the installer, Roger, arrived with a helper, and they spent several hours doing a fine job of installing a Midea air conditioner in the window.

Views inside and out; Below: before and after installation of drainage hose (nearly invisible)

After lunch, we took some BF&C in a little plastic bag, and drove to give it to Denise (who has a little toe that is black and threatened with amputation) at her place of work, the International Market Place, in Waikiki. Bruce dropped Sara off and found a place to turn around and came back and picked her up. We were back in Kaneohe in a matter of an hour or so. Bruce napped while Sara watched the MAHA movie first episode with Helaman. After supper, we are watching a movie that claims to be a true story, Shadowlands (1993).
