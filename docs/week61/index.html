Hawaii Laie Mission — Week 61

Bruce and Sara Conrad, 2026

Sunday (August 16, 2026) church was canceled because of tropical storm Lala and we spent the entire day without power, which went off at about 6am. Keeping cool and trying to open the fridge as quickly and infrequently as possible was the order of the day. Fresh from the ward preparedness evening yesterday, we filled the bathtub, kitchen sink, and four buckets with water in case the power outage extended  long enough that the water treatment plant fails—so we would be prepared if that were to happen.

Back row: aloe 1, mystery plant, aloe 2; Front: bucket collection collecting rain water

Even with the windows open it was so hot and (at 91% humidity) muggy. Sara was worried about our across-the-way neighbor and went over after dark to see if she was okay; we will be charging her phone for her, using Helaman's orange Jackery battery pack. She had a nice long talk with Barbara who has lived here 25 years. We fell asleep.

Monday morning, the power came on at 12:20am, waking us up, and we fiddled around with things before going back to bed. Cell phone having trouble connecting for some reason.

A hardwired smoke detector is buzzing and unresponsive to button pushing and can't seem to be disconnected; have we mentioned how much we hate smoke detectors? This buzzing is loud enough to be annoying now that we're awake, but not loud enough to wake us from a sound sleep, so just a complete fail. At 2am it is nice and cool with the ceiling fan going, but neither of us can seem to settle down enough to sleep. Watching the movie The Arrival (1996) worked for Sara, and soon after it ended, for Bruce.

Sara woke early to take Barbara's cell phone back to her, while Bruce slept in. We opened up the windows and cooled the place off about three degrees during breakfast, but now we have the windows closed and A/C and dehumidifier going for the rest of the heat of the day. We aim to keep the interior temperature at 84° and the humidity around 50% (above 70% it starts setting off the smoke detector).

Since this morning, connection has been really slow; not sure how a power outage could cause that. In fact, the complex-provided WiFi failed to provide Internet access, so Bruce is using the hotspot, and even that is excruciatingly slow. Between this today, and having nothing at all yesterday, makes us realize just how much we depend on constant connectivity.

We went into the stake center to try the computers there, but they wouldn't even turn on. While looking for breaker boxes, Bruce heard a regular dripping sound coming from the technical room across the hall; sure enough there was a leak of water dripping onto a document stand and from there splashing onto keyboards and mice and remote controls and a couple of personal computers. Long story short, we called a few people and worked a bit to move things out of the way and catch the drip in a garbage can. When Justin arrived to take charge, we left and drove to Safeway to buy lunch. Then napped and vacuumed the carpets. Sara had been waiting for mayonnaise to arrive via Amazon and it did! Disconcerting that it is Amazon brand mayo.

Jasmine baptism at 6pm. It was a beautiful service, with a sweet spirit present, as Jasmine was baptized by Bishop Kaluhiokalani, and confirmed by the husband of her friend who gave the talk on baptism. Another friend (in red dress) gave the talk on the gift of the Holy Ghost.

In the background (in the kitchen) one can just glimpse Brother Stanger, who confirmed Jasmine a member of the church, and his wife, Sister Stanger, who gave the talk on baptism.

Those pictures were taken after the entire service. In the middle, between the baptism and the confirmation, while Namele played hymns on the piano, everyone wrote a card for Jasmine. Sara told a story that Bruce hadn't heard before (despite nearly 22 years of marriage).

"I am 67 years old. I made a decision when I was 8 years old to follow the spirit of the Holy Ghost. I was lost in the woods. I came into a clearing and could NOT remember how I had entered this meadow. I prayed to God to lead me back to my family. I was very frightened. I listened to my friend the Holy Ghost and he led me out of the meadow to my family. I KNOW that when we follow the promptings that we will be blessed to meet with GOD & CHRIST in our lives whether in this life or in eternity. Sister Sara Anne Conrad"

Brother Stanger, Namele Fauatea, Sister Stacey playing violin, Sister Beaty, Brother Tomita

The closing hymn was I Will Walk with Jesus, with Sister Stacey playing the first verse and chorus, and all of us singing the remaining verses and choruses.

Sara drove us home (since it was after dark), stopping by Popeyes since we had a coupon and we tried their blackened chicken strips, which are very good, and which Sara can eat because they are not breaded. Parking was fun, in tight quarters, which we attempted because of the long line at the drive thru. At home we enjoyed the cool of the evening. Bruce took advantage of the Internet working again to update a blog post responding to some comments and suggestions that Phil had emailed. He worked on this post, and was also able to record the picos for the second evening update (each day, he does a manual operation after 8pm and again between 9:10pm and 11pm (at 3am Mountain Time, if he hasn't completed the second update, the pico engine wakes up and emails him 27 hours of temperature readings (so they won't be lost), so he strives to avoid that by completing it before it happens)). Then, having spent about an hour profiting from the newly available Internet access, he joined Sara and we fell asleep to a movie based on true events a novel, Before and After (1996).

Tuesday morning at about 1:30am, Bruce woke up overheated and asked if it would be okay if he turned on the overhead fan, and Sara quipped, "Good luck with that." Oh, the power was off again. He went back to sleep while Sara, using the hotspot on his phone, watched something on her computer. In the morning, he awoke spontaneously at precisely 6:30am to write these two paragraphs and reset the clocks. Looks like the power had been off for about an hour. This time, the Internet access wasn't affected.

While Bruce is working on a couple of blog posts, Helaman invited Sara to go with him for breakfast at Pan's Kitchen. They returned saying everything was closed, with no electricity. We got ready and attended district council. On the way home, we stopped to see if the food court was open in the mall, and informed the young missionaries by text that it was. Then we went to Ross and purchased a small pot that will replace the one we have which has such a rounded bottom that it is difficult to use on the stove top. We got the weekly newspaper, and Bruce finished the sudoku puzzle first; we are still working on the crossword puzzle. Also, naps.

In the evening we watched the monthly senior devotional, from the Laie visitor center, thanks to a Zoom meeting. It was an interesting presentation on our constitution and the principles upon which it was founded. We watched the end of the movie from last night, then fell asleep.

Wednesday morning, Bruce awoke at 6:27 to a beautiful sunny day in Hawaii (of course), and paid our tithing online, checked email, etc. We drove to Honolulu to pick up Denise and take her to pick up medication. Then we drove to the Costco gas pumps downtown and it was so busy that we then drove to the Costco store in Hawaii Kai. We ate lunch there and then shopped. Home to Kaneohe by 1:15pm. The house, left open to cross ventilation, had reached 86° inside, so we shut the windows and turned on the air conditioning. Later in the evening, the windows are open again and we are cool without A/C. Sara is off visiting Barbara. Someone in our zone sent a picture of the Kaneohe sky tonight. Watched Billy Bathgate (1991).

Thursday morning dawned sunny and calm. No wind at all, which is unusual on this the windward side of the island. We attended the zoom meeting that is mission-wide and called The Iron Rod. We stopped it just before the end because we needed to hit the road to arrive at the endodontist in time for Sara's appointment. They took a panoramic scan of her jaws and it was very interesting to see what is going on there. Her return appointment is in two weeks. Bruce made an appointment with a regular dentist for his painful tooth number fifteen, next Tuesday at five p.m. We got ripped off at the Safeway in Kailua so won't be returning there anytime soon. Sara ate at Panda Express, while Bruce ate the Safeway meal such as it was. Naps.

Our ward is going to have a choir for ward conference next month, and we have been invited to be a part of it. The first rehearsal was supposed to be Sunday after church but there wasn't church, so it will be tonight instead, overlapping our shift at the family history library. Choir was challenging as we needed to learn one verse and the chorus in Hawaiian. No one came needing help with family history. Sara took a bath while Bruce had a nice long talk with Helaman.

Friday morning came and also the transcript showing that Sara got an A in her Finance 300 class through BYU Pathways Worldwide. It is from BYU Idaho which is a bit confusing. Also, it is an unofficial transcript which we don't know if that is sufficient. So an inquiry has been sent, and she is on hold on the phone with enrollment services, who after a long time with the obnoxious hold "music" told her to call the BGS office (who were the ones who told her to call enrollment services in the first place (phone tag)). Now she's back with BGS who transferred her to their transcript department where the guy is helpful but transfers her over to the Pathways office (more obnoxious hold noise (which she handles by turning the volume way down (which means she has to pay careful attention))). He (Charles) couldn't get them on the phone and ended up having to email Pathways with the question and then we'll have to wait for them to answer. At least he is willing to write the message for her and send it for her. The initial reply was that they "don't do that" but they forwarded the request on to someone else, hopefully with more capability. We understand their desire to have self-service—for people to do things themselves—but surely there is someone who can just press a button and it will be done. It is so frustrating. Bruce wrote to Elder Hollingsworth to see if he can help.

Three cases of Books of Mormon were delivered to our door step; the delivery man's face was covered in sweat as we thanked him for carrying them, one at a time, up our fifteen steps. Sara fixed a superb supper and we retired for the night, watching some forgettable movie.

Saturday began with a phone call from Jessica in the wee hours, and we went back to sleep. The day dawned brightly but overcast with the usual bird songs and squawks. Bruce let Sara sleep while he wrote these sentences. We went over to Barbara's lanai (on the ground floor) and carried all of her boxes to the trash; Sara broke them down and Bruce carted them. Shower and bath and laundry today. We worked a little bit on the song we are going to need to learn in Hawaiian but couldn't remember exactly how the extra syllables lined up with the notes. We learned that Malu States of our bishopric is a specialist, a master actually, in teaching the Hawaiian language. Bruce is trying intermittent fasting (hoping to lose weight) and is not eating today. We spilled a bowl of hot soup on the bed and so are doing more laundry.
