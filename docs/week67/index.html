Hawaii Laie Mission — Week 67

Bruce and Sara Conrad, 2026

Sunday (September 27, 2026) we awoke to find that there had been a power outage, but everything seems calm as normal—not overly windy, despite hurricane Nolo. Church was fast and testimony meeting this week, because of General Conference next weekend. The testimonies were all thought-provoking and uplifting. The conducting councilor had experienced a tragic family loss yesterday, so that was especially moving. We ate quickly and returned to the chapel for a baptism service.

Shaiann: left with Sisters Dushku and Broderick, far right with Sisters Dushku and Stacy

We spoke briefly with her father, a landscaper who is not a member, afterwards on the lawn, but didn't partake of the refreshments. It was a moving ceremony. Fun to see Sister Stacy who was visiting from her new assignment in Laie. These two sets of sisters have been teaching Shaiann for many months and they were so happy to see her baptized at last. In the evening, we had talks with Helaman, and watched some less-than-memorable movies to lull us to sleep.

Monday morning, we found our LTK 101 lesson, in which we learned about the first 13 of the 36 exercises. It was said to be in front of Macy's but there wasn't such a store in the mall (it was where it used to be, which anyone who had lived here any length of time would have known, but we of course did not). After it ended around nine, we learned that the group meets every other month for lunch; this month it was going to be brunch in the food court when things opened at ten. Sara didn't want to walk back to the car, so Bruce drove to sell bottles and cans ($4.60) while Sara stayed in the food court with the group, which Bruce joined at about 9:30. We talked and met people, and when the various shops opened, we all got food and ate together. The group planned the next outing in November, which will be taking the train across the island.

Back home, we had lunch and then drove to Kaaawa with Helaman. We looked through the two adjacent apartments and then Helaman and Bruce walked to the beach park. It took only five minutes to walk from one apartment door, across the highway to the edge of the ocean.

Looking due South (at the left) then swinging around West until looking due North (at the right)

It is mostly rocks along the edge of the ocean, but at the north end past the palm trees, there is a nice little bit of sandy beach, from which this second faux panorama looking roughly south.

The iconic mountain north of Kualoa Regional Park is seen here in the distance from behind

Helaman informed us that Kaaawa is the only town in [the United States of] America whose name has three A's in a row. On the way back to home in Kaneohe, we stopped at the roundabout and purchased an ice-cold coconut and some apple bananas, plus freebies.

Tuesday morning, Bruce got up just before sunrise and finished a book while watching the sky; Sara meanwhile was shelling hard-boiled eggs as she is preparing deviled eggs as a treat for district council. We were a few minutes early to LTK in the mall. Home to shower and get ready for the rest of the morning. We had roles to play in the role playing as part of the district council training. Sisters Brenders and Oates did the training and brought in crepes which were very good; we provided the bananas purchased yesterday. We returned home and rested. The puzzles came and Sara finished both first; we were tied in our crossword puzzle scores, both missing eleven words. Watching Father Goose (1964) to put us to sleep.

Wednesday morning, we got up and prepared for the morning. Bruce finished filling out a form required by his Canadian dollar retirement fund managers and got it ready for mailing. We prepared for an expedition including the morning LTK session, driving to Swanzy beach, showering and doing some dishes in Kaaawa. Taking a break from the dishes we shopped in Hauula and Laie, then stopped for lunch at Keneke's in Punaluu on the way back to Kaaawa.

Sara and Bruce having lunch; Sara's artwork on each fry: bed of catsup with a drizzle of mayo

When we got home to Kaneohe, we found a message from the elders sharing the photo taken with our president a couple of weeks ago at Zone Conference so we are including it hereabove. Now watching All at Sea (2010) to bring sleep—didn't work—perhaps Our Town (1940) will. Did.

Thursday, Bruce set an alarm for six to participate in a zoom meeting with many of his Hancock cousins. It ended just in time for us to drive to the mall for LTK 101 part two, then back to the apartment for breakfast, and then resting a little. Sara put together a delicious-smelling soup.

We note that today is the first day of a new month. Welcome to October 2026, meaning that we have three full months left in our missionary assignment. Our ward has seminary security this month, but Bruce hasn't been able to find the sign-up sheet. When we got to the chapel for our family history shift, Bruce asked Justin about the seminary sheet and he shared it with him. Sara is working on the paper for her last class, while Bruce is preparing for a set of sister missionaries who will be coming at 7:45 to print some things. They found a twenty page conference activities book and printed out two copies of it for Mehana and Makena.

Sara, Sister Oates, Sister Brenders, with Bruce behind the camera in family history room

We are watching No Sad Songs For Me (1950), a melodrama. Sara fell asleep during it and Bruce shortly thereafter.

Friday morning we got up and made it to the LTK session just in time. Afterwards, Sara started shopping at Safeway while Bruce went back to the apartment for his phone and the list. We did end up saving ten dollars on a stack of paper plates. Resting just a bit after breakfast. Going up to Kaaawa when we're rested, where we went into the ocean, showered, and did some general cleaning before returning to the apartment in Kaneohe. The sisters will be stopping by at 8pm to pick up some calling cards (of which we received 500 a week or so ago).

Sister Millward, Bruce, Sara, Sister Tupou picking up some calling cards and wishing us well

We are watching Notorious (1946) to hopefully put us to sleep tonight.

Saturday morning, the alarm went off in time for us to watch the first session of General Conference. Breakfast, shower, then second session, lunch, non-overlapping naps, and Sara cleaned our box fan with a little help from Bruce, and baked a chicken. We gobbled down a bit of roast chicken and then went to a baptism service for Tay, who is Russian. Some candids.
