Hawaii Laie Mission — Week 69

Bruce and Sara Conrad, 2026

Sunday (October 11, 2026) is a sunny morning, and not too hot at 79° at 8am, a couple of hours after sunrise.

Early in the morning, then shortly before sunset, Keahi a Kahoe is always there, often visible

Helaman left around 6am for the second of two days of training required here to operate a motorcycle; it is being held in Laie. Yesterday we had been a little worried about him because he hadn't slept well and it was a longer day than he had expected.

Sara is starting a new medication and it is upsetting her stomach, so Bruce brought her home after sacrament meeting. It was a beautiful service, including the homecoming talk of a sister missionary, preceded by a talk from a young married man who had actually spent his last transfer in the same mission the sister was returning from. Between the talks, she and her younger sister sang a beautiful rendition of Learn of Me in two part harmony. The returning sister wore an elaborate lei and a crown of flowers. There was a great spirit in the chapel. So much so that the bishop had us end without the closing hymn, and we got to the other meetings in time. Bruce returned after dropping Sara off and passed around the signup sheet for missionary meals; liked priesthood meeting. After the block, he remained for choir practice.

Monday started with LTK, then we went to U-Haul about a truck, then to the ocean, then showers, and naps. Helaman moved our outdoor things—including the plants—to Kaaawa. We fell asleep to the movie Manipulated (2019).

Tuesday morning we woke up spontaneously before sunrise, lazing in bed for a while. LTK and then breakfast, during which the power went out for a few minutes, so we had to reset all the clocks. We were on time to district council, which was very inspiring, the last of this transfer.

Guillermo & Garner, Dushku & Broderick, Oates & Brenders, Millward & Tupou, Walker & Hess

As per usual, after the opening hymn, prayer, and missionary standards moment, each set of missionaries stands and reports on their week: the high, the low, the miracle, friends and new members. Above is what an instant of that looks like. After district leader Garner gave the training he invited us all to testify for a couple minutes each. It was so inspiring. Bruce jumped up first and told the start of his story of the  one convert of his young mission; Sara was last before the DL had the final word; she talked about writing out the Book of Mormon (her story).

The young missionaries had a lunch appointment right after, but before they rushed off, Sister Tupou took a couple of group pictures. We said fond farewells to Sisters Tupou, Dushku, and Oates, and to Elder Garner, all of whom are leaving the district. We drove straight to U-Haul, got a truck, and took a load of stuff to Kaaawa. Sara and the elders had visited with Barbara across the way after helping load, and then brought over a huge under-the-cabinet microwave that had never been used and set it in the back of the box truck. Upon our return to Kaneohe, Bruce dropped Sara off to get our car, then tried to give the microwave  to Goodwill near the mall, but they couldn't take it and anyway were closing at 5pm; they recommended the Goodwill near the fire station, but they also could not take it and recommended Salvation Army in Kailua. Bruce went to fill up with gas at Shell before going to the U-Haul franchisee where he and Sara moved it into the back of our car. Bruce swept out the truck, we settled the bill, and he backed it into its spot. As we were unloading things at the bottom of our stairs, we met a neighbor, Leon, who took the microwave off our hands! We had a nice long talk with him, and are very grateful that it might get a new home (he will try to give it away to a contractor he knows, or he will take it to the dump for us). Leon was raised in the church until his mother went inactive when he was still a child, in Los Angeles; he told us how he ended up in Hawaii, which he loves, and how his brother married, became a Catholic and is raising six children very devoutly. An interesting and friendly man. We were done after 6pm, and exhausted, fell into bed.

Wednesday was a go to the dentist day for Bruce, starting with a permanent crown fitting for tooth 15 from Dr. Yugawa at 9am. We just came home and rested until it was time to go for an appointment at 3:30pm with Dr. Nogueira. Both dentists are in the same Pali Palms Plaza in Kailua, so there was a bit of driving involved in our day. We ended up at Moonga in Kaneohe.

Three attempts at a selfie of us and our seafood and crispy salmon skin meals; we ate happily

Thursday morning, we went to LTK at the mall again and got quite the workout, as usual. We laundered the window curtains and rested. The sisters phoned us to see if we could join them.

Sister Brenders, Elder and Sister Conrad, Sister Kakakovikaerau in front of his house

During our evening shift at the family history library, we figured out how to convert the five assignments for Sara's class to PDF files and emailed them to the BGS program. Upon our return home, Sara submitted all of them through Canvas and she is finished with the last class, the capstone of her program. Now, we just have to wait for the grade to be posted and the diploma to be delivered. We tried watching The Forgotten (2004) but it was so strange and interesting that it didn't put us to sleep; however we fell asleep shortly after it ended.

Friday morning, we decided to meet Helaman for breakfast at Pan's Kitchen. But we ended up at a different place, Café Kalawe, which was just fine, and we enjoyed a chat with the young waiter who told us about his recent degree obtained in California (and how he found life so expensive there). We drove to LTK and Helaman joined us, which was fun. Then home to pack up some things. Bruce drove to get a tank of gas and sell bottles and cans ($5.15), then home again. We drove both cars to Kaaawa and hung out. Bruce went with Helaman to get a couple of pizzas he had ordered from Dominos in Hauula. Trying Harry Black and the Tiger (1958) to put us to sleep; looks like it might work. We got an email from the dean at BYU Hawaii thanking us for our update and saying he will write to our stake presidents in support of our application for a brief service mission to follow this mission, during their winter semester.

Saturday morning we took in some ocean. Bruce got entangled with one long tentacle of a small jellyfish and was able to disconnect it and suffered no lasting ill effects. We bathed and changed and went to a beautiful baptism service in Keolu at 10am. Then home for naps.

Newly baptized Jastyn (under the exit sign) about to sit down with ward young women

There were several missionaries from the Laie stakes in attendance, including a set of elders who are teaching Jastyn's older sister in Kaaawa; Sara spent some time talking with that sister.
