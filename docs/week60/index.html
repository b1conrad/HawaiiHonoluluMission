Bruce and Sara Conrad, 2026

Monday we woke up with the windows open and didn't have to turn on the air conditioners until around 9am. We are getting ready to attend district council, on Monday this week.

District council was very uplifting today. After, we gave away the remaining Books of Mormon and drove to Whole Foods in Kailua for lunch. Sara was able to find some broccoli sprouts there and also purchased their pepperoni pizza. Then we deposited the refund check for the window removal last week, and got some books at the library, and drove home. Here we turned on the air conditioners and dehumidifier and the place is already cooling nicely. Such a great change from our first year living here. Mike texted us about mail and deposited the Canadian dollar check which finally arrived (having been mailed July 3rd). Bruce read while Sara prepared salads and a soup.

Tuesday, we were up before 6am, getting ready to drive to the temple. Doing sealings with the young missionaries of the district was delightful, although completely exhausting, both spiritually and physically; we performed 20 sealings of sons and 28 sealings of daughters to parents. Home to lunch and naps. Sara finished the weekly sudoku first, while Bruce finished the crossword puzzle first. Supper and resting. Falling asleep to Emperor (2012).

Bruce drove cans and bottles to recycle ($4.85), and when he returned he read through some work that Phil Windley is sharing with him; very interesting. Fell asleep to a movie, The Endless Game (1989).

Sara spent a long time on the phone and on-line trying to find a dentist covered by our insurance plan. Begin rant on insurance. Insurance is a form of gambling. Since not every dentist is covered by every plan, there is meta gambling going on too: when you select a plan you are gambling that you'll be able to find a suitable dentist covered by that plan. By buying insurance, you are gambling that you will need it; the insurance company is gambling that you won't (or that they'll be able to weasel out of paying out anyway). It's a horrible racket, and anyone involved in any way in providing it should quit immediately and get a productive job. I want simplicity: get a service and pay for it—no middlemen. End of rant.
