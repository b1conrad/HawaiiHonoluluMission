Hawaii Laie Mission — Week 71

Bruce and Sara Conrad, 2026

Sunday (October 25, 2026) we woke up separately; Sara had been awake a few times during the night, and Bruce had had indigestion and gone out to sleep in the recliner for a few hours.

After breakfast we did scripture study and got to church just as the meeting started. The primary children put on an excellent program, which was followed by a great Sunday School lesson from Scott, then priesthood meeting. After that we met with the Longs to give her a priesthood blessing. We do so value their friendship.

Then after choir practice, we drove home, where Sara prepared lunch. Naps. We watched the movie K-19: The Widowmaker (2002). Preparing for an early morning tomorrow.

Monday morning very early, we left for seminary security. Bruce is sitting at the entrance reflecting on yesterday; as he was preparing to give Florence a blessing he had stumbled upon Alma 12:33, which neatly summarizes the plan of redemption in one verse: if we will repent (turn to God) and harden not our hearts, He will have mercy on us through his Son. Since her pain gives her difficulty getting to sleep, after the blessing we shared with her Sara’s remedy in 2 Nephi 9:52: “Let your hearts rejoice.” Expressing gratitude at night (instead of counting sheep, count blessings). He notes this morning that both of these passages call for the exercise of agency—we choose and the consequence follows. He picked up Sara and we drove to LTK at the mall, then back for breakfast, laundry, and drove back to Kaaawa. Bruce walked through the garden walkway to the post office to send state tax check to SLC and tax return to Ogden.

From chez nous towards the street, the gate at the end, and just outside the gate looking north

It is a pleasant walk through the green, though nervewracking walking along the highway about a hundred yards to the left. Swanzy Beach Park is across the highway from there. Naps.

Tuesday morning we left at 7:20 to participate in LTK, then home to eat breakfast and rush off to district council meeting. Sister Millard attended virtually because Sister Lam is ill (as is Sister Daniels of the same apartment (so half of that one)). We had a very nice zoom visit with the Bassetts who will be replacing us as MLS missionaries in the Kaneohe Zone sometime in February. At 5:30pm, Irving Chun came and picked us up to drive to a presentation in wealth management followed by a buffet dinner. Home around nine, to learn that a power outage in Orangeville has stopped Bruce's temperature sensors from operating. Watching a movie to get to sleep, Ithaca (2015). Maybe it will work. No. The Visit (1964) maybe? Worked for Sara.

Wednesday morning, Bruce is awake at 3am with a bit of acid reflux. That, combined with sore knees (for no obvious reason), is a bit annoying. Last night he got to read Phil Windley's most recent blog post which is pretty exciting; this being a busy week however, it will be a few days before he can spend any time exploring the new territory it opens up. After the power outage in Orangeville, the sensor in the shed didn't start sending readings when power came back on. Later he checked, and it started up; phew.

Bruce is ensuring security for early morning seminary at the stake center, arriving this morning at a quarter to five and checking all of the unlocked rooms before opening the crash bars at the north end of the makai hallway. People soon started to trickle in; first the teacher of the 5:30 class, Sister Kia, then her students, then the teacher of the 6am class and her students. At 6:15, people begin to leave for the rest of their day. Bruce greets each arrival with a cheery aloha and good morning, and advises those departing to have a good day. There are several minutes of tranquility at the end of the first class until ten minutes to seven. Soon the building will be empty and he will lock up and move on to the rest of his day. He met Brother Beatty, who teaches a third class in the high council room, which he didn't know about. Bob enters the building with his own key at the door at the other end of the hallway, but his students come through the door that Bruce is "guarding." That was good to know, as is the fact that Bob is willing to lock the gate, which gets Bruce home that much sooner.

After seminary he drove to pick up Sara for LTK at the mall, then came home to bathe and do laundry. Oh, after breakfast (which could have gone without saying). Bruce had a long phone conversation with Elizabeth, and Sara one with Jessica. We stopped for chicken and sushi at a little place in Waikane just north of our favorite fruit stand. Naps. Asleep before nine due to long days back to back.

Thursday morning we were awake before four and quickly packed up so that Bruce could do his last shift of early morning seminary security. We were a little late so he finished a quick sweep of the building and got back to the one door that is used for this just as Sister Kia stepped up to the door. Speaking of the door, there was a lot of condensation on the outside, so much so that there was a small puddle of water in front of it. Bruce felt some premature nostalgia because this is the very last time he will be doing this here. The other teacher brought him some fried rice. We got home in time to bathe and dress for zone conference. We talked for a while with Elder and Sister Kaline who attended from the mission office. Our stake served rice and chicken for lunch after the group picture was taken.

Thanks to Sister Brenders for this portrait during lunch

After naps and laundry, we took our shift at the family history center, and made a plan to do temple work in a couple of weeks. We fell asleep, finishing a movie that we had started some days ago, Michael (1996).

Friday morning we are awake just after six, waiting for it to be time to go to LTK. Bruce double checked our bank accounts and noted that the Canadian dollar retirement check has cleared. We signed and mailed the 2025 state tax form. When Helaman got back from his trip to Kapolei we went out and moved the Scion so he could park at the head of the space. We decided to return the mystery plant to the Haiku Point apartment because it actually would grow into a very large tree, so it is best to leave it in the pot, says Al.

Saturday morning, Bruce walked to the shore to see if there would be a sunrise. Of course, there was, but given the cloudiness it wasn't very dramatic. He then loaded up our vacuum cleaner and drove to participate in the chapel cleaning; again, this is a last time. Our ward's next assignment isn't until January and we will no longer be in that ward then. Upon his return, he helped Sara finish cleaning and straightening up the place. She has food cooking in the crock pot, and it was very good. It has been a rainy day, and we enjoyed a quiet evening. Several loads of laundry.

Today is the last day of October.
