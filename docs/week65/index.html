Hawaii Laie Mission — Week 65

Bruce and Sara Conrad, 2026

Sunday (September 13, 2026) morning, Bruce awoke in time to go out and see the sunrise, and took some photographs from our porch, here known as a lanai.

Looking East between 6:15am and 6:20am

We arrived early at church for a run through of the hymn the choir is singing, and had a great sacrament meeting that was our yearly ward conference, with talks from our bishop, a councilor in the stake presidency and the stake president. The latter also led our Sunday School discussion, while the councilor met with the youth in the Relief Society room. We received a text message from our mission president, "I will give you a call later to explain the situation with sr missionaries and headcount as well as your current housing situation." This led us to wait while worrying about what it all might mean; likely our hopes of extending the mission so that we could work at BYU Hawaii are at risk of being dashed. We fell asleep watching The Triangle.

Monday morning we got up and left a little early for an important meeting in Honolulu, which technically was a loss, but we'll be okay going forward. Shopping afterwards at Costco and home to Kaneohe. Phone call from our president and we will have to apply for another mission in order to work at BYU Hawaii next year. Our home bishop will be opening up the application.

Last night we fell asleep to the third (and last (apparently, since there was no cliff-hanger)) part of The Triangle. Tuesday morning, Bruce was up early to work on our new mission application and write to Bishop Hughes about it. Sara finished her portion, and the Bishop it turns out was able to see it. He called the missionary department and they suggested having the mission president talk to the infield representative who can expedite it. If that doesn't work we are to let him know. Leaving for district council, Bruce captured this view of a gecko on a chair arm.

They usually move pretty quickly, so this one was posing we suppose

The district council meeting was very fine; it is always uplifting to watch as the young people missionaries bring the spirit into their work. Brother Ahuna helped them with the lesson. He and his family have performed around the world, and he brought a great deal of enthusiasm to the lesson on uniting with the members. Afterwards he showed videos of his family. We learned that Sister Brenders has lived in Lyon, France, and knows Elder Giraud-Carrier (who Bruce knew as a youth in the Paris Stake when he spoke in Versailles as a member of the stake high council).

Bruce drove to the library and sold bottles and cans ($3.50) while Sara rested and talked with her cousin David and son Eli. When he got back, the puzzles were in the mailbox (part of a weekly magazine) and we worked on those. Sara finished the sudoku in a flash. The crossword puzzle is quite hard this week, and we paused so Sara could get something to eat. When we started again, Bruce finished first. Then we attended the monthly senior devotional via zoom.

Wednesday morning very early Ammon phoned us; he is with Elizabeth who is in labor. So exciting. Getting ready for zone conference was a bit hectic, juggling breakfast, a bath for Sara, a shower for Bruce, breakfast, and a short nap for Sara. We arrived during the opening hymn, and it was an uplifting meeting. Good to see some old friends among the young missionaries.

On the way home, we stopped for gas and a pizza at the nearby (to the meeting) Costco. We received pictures from the birth of Ammon's first child (and Sara's fourteenth grandchild (which means twenty-one for Bruce (since he counts seven of Judy's as well))).

But, first come pictures from the birth of Claire (the thirteenth grandchild), on March 3rd. For some reason, we neglected to mention this happy event (during week 37 of our mission (likely because so many other things were going on closer to us)), so making up for it here. So, Sara has missed the births of two grandchildren while being away on a mission—what a sacrifice! Along with missing (in the more usual way) all of the children and grandchildren.

We fell asleep to The Librarian: Quest for the Spear (2004), then woke up about 10pm to finish it and trying again with One Life (2023). Sara woke briefly (during one of the annoying ads) to close the computer, white Bruce labors over this post (who swears the pun was unintended).

Thursday morning up and at 'em. Helaman preparing for a meeting in Honolulu. Bruce drove Sara to her next physical therapy appointment. Upon return, we enjoyed a nap. Then it was time to meet the elders for dinner, which we did at Chili Rellenos. While there, some members on vacation paid our bill! That was a very pleasant surprise. We bought them a dessert, so that was it, although it made us late to our family history shift which we skipped to talk with Helaman.

Elders Guillermo and Garner trial picture, and then at table with Elder and Sister Conrad

We returned home so that Helaman could tell us about his meeting. Watching The Librarian: Curse of the Judas Chalice put Sara to sleep. Bruce followed.

We awoke Friday morning, did the usual morning routine, and talked with Helaman about our proposed visit to Kaaawa later this morning. He is not feeling well, and the stress he is under is making him nauseous, so he won't be able to come along with us. We are looking for the herbal tea, yerba mate, which is our cure for nausia and vomiting, but apparently we are all out. We went by ourselves to look at the two apartments that we are thinking of renting for a couple of months. They are very nice (pictures another time if it works out). We got a call from our mission president who explained to us the problem with asking for a call for just three and a half months, as it would take out of consideration a slot that the missionary department has open for a year, and they don't want to lose that slot. On the other hand, they are trying to reduce the number of senior missionaries working at BYU Hawaii because of the high cost of subsidizing rent in Hawaii (and more seniors are needed in Africa where housing is much cheaper for the church (our rent in Mauritius was only $500 a month)).

Saturday morning, Bruce awoke at six and went quietly into the other room so that Sara could sleep in a bit, as it promises to be a long day. We drove to the stake center at 8am and Sara went in to the Stake Relief Society activity day, while Bruce waited in the Family History room proofreading the blog. Sara brought him a plate of food afterwards and made up a takeout box for Barbara. Sara said it was really a great meeting.

We got home for a short rest and then returned for a baptism. Two young women in the Pierce family were baptized by their father.

Then we waited outside in the car with the A/C running until it was time to go to the restaurant to meet Elder and Sister Sumsion who will be driving down from Hauula (near Laie) to meet us.
