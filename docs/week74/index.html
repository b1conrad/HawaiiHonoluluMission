Hawaii Laie Mission — Week 74

Bruce and Sara Conrad, 2026

Sunday (November 15, 2026) morning we awoke around six, and Bruce crossed the highway to watch the sunrise and feel the wind coming from the ocean. When he returned, we packed up and drove to Kaneohe, where we ate breakfast, bathed, and dressed. We got to church, taking the sacrament, hearing from Elder Petersen and Jimmie Westergard, the ward mission leader; both were good talks about coming unto Christ. We participated in the Sunday School lesson on Daniel, and Bruce accompanied a man who was visiting for the first time, so that he would know how to get to Priesthood Meeting; it turns out our bishop knows him. Afterwards, we packed up and drove back to Kaaawa. We ate the soup that Helaman had been warming up for us, and napped. In the evening, Bruce set up some cross-ventilation and it felt nice and cool.

Monday morning, awake at five, rooster crowing. Out of order: this after district council.

After Bruce got back from watching the sun rise, we packed up and drove to Kaneohe. There we put things in the fridge and drove to LTK. From there back to breakfast, bathing and dressing. Sara didn't feel well enough so Bruce went by himself to district council. He took a box of Books of Mormon and was able to give most of them away; the remainder he stashed in the family history room. He observed the reports given by each set of young missionaries.

The lesson on Christ-like attributes of patience and humility was very well-presented by Sisters Millward and Lam; it was followed by the group photo after we sang God Be with You Till We Meet Again, because Elder Hess is being transferred to Laie tomorrow.

Arriving at the apartment, he picked up Sara and we drove to the clinic to pick up Barbara and brought her back to her home across the way from us. Then we ate lunch and spent the rest of the day cleaning the apartment. We drove to Panda Express and were almost finished eating when Ben called to tell us their flight arrival time. Sara dropped Bruce off at the apartment and rushed to the airport where she arrived in time and drove home with Emily and her daughters and had a great time; meanwhile Ben and Troy picked up the rental car. By the time they got here, Bruce had finished the laundry and started to empty the dishwasher. So good to see them again. Since Bruce had set up cross-ventilation, he went back upstairs to close the window that is low enough that kids might fall through. Sara drove us home in the dark.

Tuesday morning, Bruce was up at before the crack of dawn to work on this blog post and get ready for our next temple trip. He also wrote a couple of email messages in support of our next (service) mission application; we need to obtain the number for the opportunity at BYUH so that our stake president can submit it. We left at 7:15 and drove to the temple, where we did sealings with all of the sisters in our district. That was very uplifting. Then we drove to look at a restaurant nearby, The Bistro in the Marriott hotel. Lots of seating and parking. On the way home, we stopped at a little grocery store, Tamura's Market, to check it out, and bought a gallon of milk next door at Longs. Home by ten, expecting a visit from Ben's family in an hour. Bruce waited out on the highway for them, sending them a couple of selfies to show the context.

When they arrived, he indicated parking and then walked with them to the house. After a bit, he walked with Emily and all the kids to the beach, where they had fun frolicking in the waves and digging in the sand. We found a strange thing in the water, that looked like a flower or a jelly fish or something, but it didn't act alive. We spent some time looking at the thing, and Troy swam over and around it, but no one dared touch it with their bare hands. Finally, Bruce pushed it out onto the sand with a piece of driftwood; it is a toy volcano. Seeing several shellfish attached to the piece of driftwood, he threw it back into the ocean. Kids played with the toy volcano.

The toy volcano sitting on our driveway—obviously harmless

While they were gone, Helaman came over to visit with Ben and Sara, and then Sara went out to pick up some pizzas; those hit the spot. We sat around talking or playing.

After they left for their apartment, we watched the end of the senior fireside (a monthly event) about miracles in today's world, through the power and authority of the Priesthood. Bruce then located two pictures of our district at the temple earlier today.

Sisters Lahournere, Broderick, Brenders, Fakakovikaetau, Millward, and Lam in left picture; joined by Elders Hess, Gilbert, Walker, Petersen, Guillermo, and Fonua on the right

Wednesday morning, we were awake early again, Sara at about 4 and Bruce at 5.

Sunrise pano, roughly from southeast to southwest; Ryder truck delivers mail at this time of day

We drove to pick up Ben and he joined us at LTK. From there, we drove directly to the Waipio Costco for gas and groceries. Ben took up his part and we drove the rest to Kaaawa. On the way, we stopped at Times for broccoli sprouts and other groceries; arriving we put the food away. Then we enjoyed a brief rest, showered, dressed and are now waiting for grandkids to visit again if the parents so decide. Sara cut up watermelon and did other food prep. We started on the puzzles, and Sara finished the sudoku right away. We had just started doing the crossword when Ben and family arrived. We all went to the beach (except Sara). Troy and Bruce pulled a boogie board out of an opening in the seawall, then decided to put it back in case its owner returned for it someday. Troy got really cold, so he and Bruce walked home, closely followed by Ben and Emily and the girls. We all took turns in the bathroom, rinsing off and dressing. Helaman came back over and a nice long conversation ensued. After they all left for home, we watched a football movie, then Bruce went to sleep. Sara has a painful ear.

Ben and Helaman with Clara; Troy and Helaman playing basketball; Ellie feeding chickens

Thursday we tried to decide what to do. Not feeling well and having balance problems made Sara prefer to skip the trip to LTK this morning. We let Ben make the decision for us, and he texted us that he would come with us just as we had made the go/no-go decision as a no, so we rushed out the door, and had him walk to the entrance of Haiku Point to meet us, getting us there in plenty of time. We drove home and fixed breakfast. Bruce napped. We drove to Kenekes while Ben and family drove to meet us there. Then on to the PCC, where Bruce spent an hour getting us signed up for the discount, with help from Elder Marsden and the HR people.

Us with Ben's family in The Islands, taken by Andrea; Jacob, Andrea, and Bruce outside

We went into The Islands, took the boat ride, then threw spears in Fiji, watched a music and dance presentation in Hawaii, and the fire starting and palm tree climbing demonstration from Samoa. On the way out, about 5:30, Bruce asked a random passerby to take our picture; it was Andrea, Jacob Siebachs' daughter, whom he had asked to locate Bruce—a divine appointment. Then we got discount tickets to the Gateway Buffet, which was okay, being all-you-can-eat. Sara drove the twenty minutes to Kaaawa in darkness, where we finished our movie, Flight (2012).

Friday morning, we drove to LTK—perhaps time to explain: Luk Tung Kuen is the full name. We gather at 8am in the mall to be led through the 36 exercises in the sequence. Ben came again.

Smaller group than usual, but we meet in the mall, seen here from the upper level

Afterwards, we wanted to eat at Pan's Kitchen, but they are closed for a vacation, so we just bought milk at Longs and some groceries at Safeway, dropped Ben off, and came home to make breakfast. Naps. Sara and Helaman drove into Kaneohe to meet Ben for a movie.

Center: the movie-goers; Left: chickens roosting; Right: happy baby with grandma in car

Bruce had some time alone and read and paid our monthly tithing. They got home just after dark, and Sara made a nice cream of mushroom soup for her and Bruce to share. Soon to sleep.

Saturday morning, Bruce walked between a couple of weekend camping sites to the sea wall and tried to watch the sun rise behind a distant bank of clouds. Upon his return, he peeled, seeded, and cut up a papaya we had purchased at Kenekes on Thursday, while Sara fixed breakfast. Sadly, Bruce lost a tooth while chewing the toast. Number seven is gone, broken off at the gum line! The look would be cute for a six or seven year old—not so great at seventy-six! He had planned to have a root canal for it next month, but should not have procrastinated, and maybe it could have been saved. When Ben and family arrived, Bruce drove over to their car and Ellie joined him for the drive, following them to Turtle Bay.

A faux panorama of the bay, featuring Troy, Ellie fully behind him and Ben with Emily in water

Along the way, Ellie was asking if she could have something, but Bruce didn't understand what. It turned out to be wanting the window open, which the back window doesn't. Ben jumped in after parking his car and we drove quite a ways to find a second parking spot, then we walked back to their car and picked up the rest and walked through the public access to the beach, which had sand going all the way out to chest level water. Ben and Troy spent quite a bit of time snorkeling, while Emily and Elly concentrated on making sand castles using their neat buckets that served as molds, with Emily making multiple trips down to the water's edge to get a bucketful of wet sand. Bruce struck up a conversation with a man next to them, who it turned out is a retired dentist, who admitted that he had noticed Bruce's missing number seven tooth, saying it was an occupational hazard to notice such things.

A couple of pictures of the whole family, with an inset of Ellie actually looking at the camera

We packed up and left the bay, with Ellie traveling with Bruce again following as we drove on about six more miles to a surfing beach. We got rained on just a bit, so we took shelter under some trees. Bruce said his goodbyes, hoping to get home before dark, which he did.
