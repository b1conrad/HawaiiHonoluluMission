Hawaii Laie Mission — Week 66

Bruce and Sara Conrad, 2026

Sunday (September 20, 2026) morning began early at 6:30, as Bruce got up to watch Rachel, his niece Nancy's daughter, speak in a sacrament meeting in Provo (in the Pleasant View Seventh ward (noting that his great grandfather was in the bishopric of the original Provo Pleasant View ward many years ago)). She will be serving soon in the Vietnam Hanoi Mission.

Chị Heiss sitting on the stand, then ending her talk about overcoming feelings of inadequacy

Quoting from Nancy's blog post: "Interestingly (to me), chị means older sister while the elders have Anh Cả on their name tags (which means eldest brother). I haven't ever really considered how these titles would be translated into languages that use honorifics...or specifics...relative to age. So this is how they do it in Vietnamese." [emphasis in original]

We arrived a few minutes early for our own church service (which is at 11:30am HST), and were surprised at how very many people were there. Oh, it was the farewell service for Noah, a very nice young man who will be leaving shortly for the South Africa Durban mission. Our church no longer has missionary farewell services, officially, but those of us who remember them use that term to refer to the sacrament meeting in which a departing missionary gives their last talk before leaving. Photos are not allowed in the chapel (so how did we get the pictures of Rachel's farewell service? Because it was shared with us over zoom and us taking a screenshot doesn't count because—technically—we were not actually in the chapel) so we have no pictures of the speakers, which were the younger brother and Noah, now Elder, bedecked with so many leis that it looked painful actually. Then there was a musical number by so many members of his family, Turn Your Heart to Home (picture of that group below), and Noah was front and center and it looked like he had trouble singing because he was feeling emotional. It was beautiful, and the talks were beautiful, and emotional. After the musical number, the concluding speaker was (former) Bishop Vuikadavu, who told of how after church one Sunday, Noah had hesitantly approached him asking to talk, and they stood aside and he told him that he wanted to go on a mission. This was an answer to the prayers of a (then) newly-called bishop, and in his talk he listed the youth who have recently served and who are currently serving missions, or about to.

Normally, right after such a sacrament meeting, the members sing Aloha Oe to the departing, but because of the new meeting schedule, that was delayed until after the block.

This is the family who all sang! The mother (on his right (with glasses)) accompanied them

The band before the arrival of Elder Fauatea, and showing him being celebrated with Aloha Oe

We forgot to mention that just before the meeting started, a man (the one in an orange short-sleeved shirt) sang a beautiful solo number, listed on the program as a prelude. In the second hour, there was a great Sunday School discussion about Isaiah, followed by separate discussions in the Relief Society and Priesthood meetings. We got a lot out of all of these as well, and in all it was a great Sunday, with the spirit being so strong throughout it all.

We fell asleep to The Mudlark (1950).

Monday morning, Bruce got up early and talked with Arlene on the phone for nearly an hour, wishing her a happy birthday and catching up on the past year. Then he and Sara drove to the mall and joined an exercise class at 8am. Home for showers and naps. Bruce drove to chapel.

Young missionaries playing inside; two cattle egrets hunting on the lawn outside

We had some candy to give away, so knowing they would be there and hungry for snacks, he drove over a bowl of candy. Sister Tupou promised she would bring the bowl tomorrow so it won't be lost. We worked on transcribing a recorded phone conversation and constructing some timelines. We fell asleep to the movie A Cry in the Dark (1988).

Call from Ammon just before midnight, worried about Oliver's umbilical cord which is starting to come off two days earlier than expected. We talked him through it. Getting back asleep.

Tuesday morning, Bruce awoke at 7am and quietly went into the other room to let Sara sleep a little longer. We'll be going to exercise at 8am, so it won't be long. Overnight, the sisters sent us some pictures taken before the Pierce family baptisms last Saturday; the father had just very recently been ordained a priest. We had arrived at the chapel just as the before-picture taking was disbanding, and we didn't linger for the refreshments afterwards, because we had that dinner appointment with our friends from Laie to go to next.

The whole family; Sister Oates, Makena, Brother Pierce, Mehana, Sister Brenders

We have a busy day, with district council meeting in the morning, and another root canal session for Bruce this afternoon (the turn of tooth number 14). At district council, the other district joined us for the lesson, given by the sister training leaders and zone leaders. It is the last such meeting for Elder and Sister Peterson, so they ran a jeopardy-like game.

After the meeting we returned home and napped until it was time to leave for the endodontist. Helaman came with us, Bruce was dropped off, and Sara and Helaman went shopping at Whole Foods and returned a couple of items we had ordered but weren't satisfactory. The doctor was about halfway through the tooth when they were done and came back to wait. Sadly, he wasn't able to find the fourth canal and so Bruce will have to return in a couple of weeks for the work to be finished. Helaman had ordered food from Buffalo Wings, so we picked that up on the way home and it was good. Finishing laundry and going to bed; it has been a long day. Earlier in the day, Mike reported that he mailed our new credit cards to us.

Wednesday morning, up after a night with a few interruptions. Nothing major, just that we both woke up around midnight and had trouble getting back to sleep. We got ready and arrived at the dentist in time for Bruce's appointment at 9am to get fitted with a temporary crown on tooth fifteen. The dentist was willing to work without novacaine; a relief; no pain was felt, so justified. We met with Al Poh at Zippy's in Kaneohe. Helaman missed a phone call and called back to receive some good news for all of us.

Thursday morning we were both awake before six but didn't get up right away. Sara bathed and we went to the mall for LKT exercises, then home for showers and breakfast. Early in the afternoon, Helaman drove to Honolulu to meet with Craig. We got a text message from the Bassetts who will be replacing us and wanted to ask some questions. That is a good idea, we think. For one thing, they are buying a car from a couple leaving their mission on December 17th and want to store it in our parking spot until they arrive on February 2nd (or 9th, we had been told (wondering if they are forgetting a week in the MTC)). That should work just fine. In the evening, we worked our shift in the family history room and worked on Sara's paper.

Friday we again went to the mall for LKT, did laundry, and rested. We had asked the mission secretary to order brochures for the missionaries and they arrived today by FedEx.

Saturday morning we got up and Bruce prepared a family message for our ward's blog, with editing suggestions from Sara, while she cleaned A/C and dehumidifier filters, organized some things for the day, and much more. We enjoyed a late morning nap, then bathed, showered, and dressed. We drove to Kaaawa to meet Al Poh at his home there, which is very nicely situated, surrounded by a small area of grass, flowering shrubs, and palm trees, with a view of the mountains, and across the main road from Swanzy Beach Park. Returning home, we found our new credit cards in the mailbox.
